    The job

    Send a message somebody else can read

    Make the buzzer, or a light, send SOS. Then make it send your own initials.

    The test is not that it beeps. The test is that somebody holding the chart, who has not seen your code, can write down what you sent.

    Why this is the right place to practise writing your own functions

    Morse is two things repeated: a dot and a dash. You will type them dozens of times. Anything you are about to type dozens of times is a function waiting to be written, and this is the smallest, clearest example of that in the whole course.

    Band 5 Part 5 showed you how. This page does not show you again.

    Think first

    The timing is the whole specification

    10 min

    Morse is not “short beep, long beep”. It is a set of exact ratios, and if you get them wrong nobody can read it, which is the only test that matters.

    ThingHow long
    A dot1
    A dash3
    Gap between the parts of one letter1
    Gap between two letters3
    Gap between two words7

    Pick what 1 is worth in thousandths of a second, and everything else follows. Two hundred is a good place to start. Then a dot is 200, a dash is 600, and a letter gap is 600.

    The gap everybody forgetsThe silence inside a letter and the silence between letters are different lengths, and that is the only thing telling a listener where one letter stops. Get those two the same and you send one endless letter that nobody can split up. This is the bug to expect.

    The letters you need for SOS: S is three dots. O is three dashes.

    Build

    Build it, then hand it to somebody

    25 min
    WiringOne buzzer, or one light with its resistor. Band 5 Part 4 for the buzzer, Band 1 Part 5 for the light. That is the entire circuit.
    Either kind of buzzer works here, with one change if yours is activeMorse needs sound and silence rather than a particular note, so both kinds do the job. If yours is passive, use tone and noTone, and Band 5 Part 4's bare wiring is right. If yours is active, digitalWrite is enough — but put a 220 ohm resistor in its pin leg, as Band 3 does, because an active buzzer pulls about thirty thousandths of an amp and a pin is built for twenty.

    Not sure which you have? Fit the resistor. It costs a little volume and nothing else.
    The check, and it is a personWrite out the chart for the three letters you are sending. Hand it to somebody who has not watched you build this. Run it. They should be able to write down your three letters. If they cannot, your gaps are wrong, and no amount of looking at your own code will show you that.
    If there is nobody to hand it toRecord it on your phone. Watch it back tomorrow, with the chart, having half forgotten what you sent. That is a surprisingly honest test, because by then you are a stranger to it.
    The shape, if you are stuck
    void dot()  { /* on for one unit, off for one unit  */ }
void dash() { /* on for three units, off for one unit */ }
void letterGap() { /* silence, and how long? */ }

    Notice that dot and dash each end with their own gap built in. That is a decision, not the only way, and it makes the letter gap shorter than you might expect. Work out by how much.

    Change it

    Two ways further

    Bronze
    Send a whole word, with the seven-unit word gap in it, and have a second person read it back. Then halve your unit and see at what speed they stop being able to.
    Silver
    Go the other way: a button you tap, and the serial monitor prints a dot for a short press and a dash for a long one. You will have to decide where short stops and long starts, and then measure your own tapping to choose it. That is Band 4's calibration lesson arriving in a place nobody expects it. (You need millis to time a press, so come back to this one after Band 5 Part 7. You will also need a button and its wiring from Band 3 Part 3.)
    Done

    Keep

    • Your chosen unit length, and what a dash and each gap came out as
    • What your reader wrote down, next to what you actually sent
    • Your functions, and one sentence on what you would have typed without them
